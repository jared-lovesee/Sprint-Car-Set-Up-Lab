How to use

  • Left-drag to rotate, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom. The view buttons jump to set angles.
  • Setup: change weight, torsion bars, scales and lateral and long g. The car rolls, pitches and heaves, and the disc under each tire grows with its load.
  • Drive the corner loops the car through braking, turn-in, mid-corner and exit so you can watch the loads change. Motion ×5 makes small body movements easier to see.
  • Kinematics → Front: move the three radius rods, the track bar and the steering, then use Heave, Roll or Sweep. Watch camber, bump steer and the roll center.
  • Kinematics → Rear: index the birdcages, move the top radius rods and the Jacobs ladder holes. Watch the rear roll center, rear steer, anti-squat and the bar arm motion ratio.
  • Dynamics: set the body’s heave, roll and pitch and watch the car follow. The charts show toe, camber, caster, roll centres, rear steer, wheelbase and birdcage clocking through whichever one you plot against.
  • Dashed lines on the charts are the car as modeled. Orange numbers show which parts changed length.
  • Saved setups: save, load and compare side by side; export one to a file to share it.
  • Click a section heading to fold it away. The camera remembers your angle on each tab.
  • Hover any underlined term for a short definition.

Assumptions to check

Taken from your CAD, not assumed. Every pivot is the center of a rod-end ball fitted to the exported part, so the hardpoints are exact rather than eyeballed. The kingpin axes come from the spindles' kingpin bores: caster is 19.6° on the LF and 23.1° on the RF, KPI 5.4 and 2.3°, and camber as drawn is −2.68° LF and +1.99° RF with the steering turned about 1.9° to the right. Front tires 13.03 in radius, LR 15.06, RR 16.14 (6.7 in of stagger).

Three front radius rods, which is how a sprint car is built. The left front runs a lower rod only: that rod holds the axle fore and aft, and the right upper rod is what controls the axle's tilt. With the track bar that is four links on the beam axle, exactly the two degrees of freedom it needs, and it is why the two sides give different anti-dive.

The front torsion arm's link to the axle is not in the export. The arm reaches from the bar to a point 2 in behind the axle and stops; whatever joins it to the axle is missing. The model treats the arm tip as riding with the axle at that point, which sets the motion ratio (about 0.99 both sides). A real link would change it a little.

The Jacobs ladder's straps run to a triangle, not to the axle. The triangle is pinned to the right-rear hub, and that pin is the whole trick: it gives the ladder a joint of its own, so the axle can heave while both straps hold their length — the triangle just swings on the pin, about 13° at full travel. So the triangle's swing is solved as an unknown here and the two straps go in as real length constraints. Where the strap lines cross is the rear roll center. As drawn the ladder holds the axle within 0.03 in of centered over ±3 in of travel, which is the reason to run one instead of a track bar.

Rear jacking. The ladder triangle swings on its pin, so the two strap forces and the pin force must all pass through where the straps cross. The push therefore runs along the line from that crossing to the pin, and its rise over run sets the rear jacking, the same way the track bar's angle sets the front.

Birdcage clocking is the angle of the line from the torsion arm mount up to the radius rod mount, off vertical in side view. The cage is one piece and the torsion arm is a fixed length, splined to its bar and free only to slide along it, so the cage turns to wherever the top rod puts it: clocking is set by turning the rod end. The clocking slider solves the rod length for you and the two always agree. Clocking moves anti-squat, rear steer and the bar arm motion ratio, so the effective bar rate too.

Stagger sizes the LR tire. The slider is the circumference difference, RR minus LR; the RR is left at what the CAD drew and the LR is sized off it, which is how you change it on the car. Everything follows from there: a staggered axle cannot sit flat on the ground level, so the rear has to tilt, which moves the settled ride height, the rear roll centre and the corner weights. The tire in the 3D changes diameter with it. Stagger vs corner still compares what you have set against what a locked rear needs for the corner radius.

Rates. Torsion bar rate at the arm = GJ/L ÷ arm², J = πd⁴/32, G = 11.5 million psi. Wheel rate = that times the motion ratio squared, with the motion ratio measured off the linkage. Roll stiffness = (kL+kR)·(track/2)² per axle; there are no sway bars on this car, the torsion bars do all of it. Load transfer per axle = sprung W·Ay·hrc/t + K·φ/t + unsprung W·Ay·rtire/t. Roll, both roll centers and the front jacking are solved together, because jacking raises the body, which moves them again.

Not modeled. Shocks (every moment is a steady state, so damping and the timing of the weight transfer do not show). Bump rubbers. Tire growth. Birdcage and rod-end compliance. The wing is a load you set, not an aero model. Balance uses a tire whose friction falls with load (μ = 1.5 − 0.0004·Fz) and ignores camber, stagger and rear steer. Stagger needed = rear tire circumference × rear track ÷ corner radius, for a locked rear.

How this is solved Both ends are a rigid axle on links, so there is one solver and both ends use it: set the axle's heave and roll against the chassis, then solve the rest of its position from the link lengths. The front beam axle has three radius rods and a track bar — four links, which leaves exactly the heave and roll we set. Steering is a drag link from the pitman arm to the LF steering arm and then a tie rod across to the RF, and because the pitman arm is bolted to the chassis the front steers itself as the axle moves: that is the front bump steer, and most of it comes from the track bar scrubbing the axle sideways. Camber to the ground does not change in roll, because a beam axle stays square to the track — what changes is camber relative to the chassis. The rear has a top radius rod and a torsion bar arm per side, both on a birdcage that turns on the axle tube, plus the Jacobs ladder, which goes in as a lateral constraint only because its triangle is pinned to the right-rear hub. The birdcage rotations are solved along with the axle, and indexing a birdcage turns its mounting ears on the tube before any of that. Anti-dive and anti-squat are measured off the solved motion, as the fore and aft travel of the contact patch per inch of vertical travel, times wheelbase over CG height (and the brake share at the front) — which is the same thing as the side-view link angle but works for any linkage, including a front end with one rod on the left and two on the right. The front roll center is where the track bar crosses the centerline; the rear is where the two ladder straps cross. When you move a mounting hole the axle stays at its ride-height position and the link length changes to fit, the way it does on the car.
